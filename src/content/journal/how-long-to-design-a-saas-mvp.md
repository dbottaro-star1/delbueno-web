For a focused SaaS MVP with one core workflow and one main user role, plan on 2 to 4 weeks of design once the scope is agreed. Every extra role, integration or workflow adds time, and the slowest part is rarely drawing screens: it is getting decisions made.

That range assumes the design team works in short loops with you. At delbueno™ Studio, kickoff happens the same day scope is agreed and first high-fidelity concepts arrive within 48 hours, so the weeks go into refining the right thing instead of waiting for a big reveal.

## What happens in those weeks?

A typical rhythm for a single-workflow MVP:

- **Days 1 and 2:** agree on the one job the MVP must do, who does it and what "done" looks like. First concepts of the core screen.
- **Week 1:** the main flow end to end, from sign-up to first value, shown to a few target users or tested on sales calls.
- **Weeks 2 and 3:** empty states, errors, settings and the edge cases engineers will ask about, in a clickable prototype.
- **Final days:** a handoff with components, states and notes developers can build from, then design review while the first build comes together.

If you are still deciding what belongs in the MVP, our [UX design for startups guide](/ux-design-for-startups/) covers what matters at pre-seed and seed.

## What makes it take longer?

- **Moving scope.** Changing the core job halfway through restarts the work.
- **More roles.** An admin, a member and a viewer mean three versions of many screens.
- **Data-heavy screens.** Dashboards, permissions and alerts need real or realistic data to design well.
- **Slow feedback.** A week waiting for comments is a week lost. One decision maker who answers within a day keeps things moving.
- **Regulated domains.** Fintech and healthtech add review steps and copy that legal has to approve.

## How do you speed it up without cutting quality?

Write a one-page brief before kickoff, line up two or three real users to test with, and agree that the MVP solves one job well. Design onboarding as part of the MVP, not after it: our post on [SaaS onboarding UX patterns](/saas-onboarding-ux-patterns/) shows why the first minutes decide activation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a SaaS MVP be designed in one week?

A single core flow can reach a testable prototype in about a week when scope is fixed and feedback is fast. A full MVP with onboarding, settings and edge cases usually needs more.

### Should design be finished before development starts?

No. Engineers can start on the data model and infrastructure while design settles the main flow, then build screens as they are approved. Running both in parallel is usually faster than a strict handoff.

### Do AI design tools shorten the timeline?

They speed up exploration and variations. They do not speed up the decisions about who the user is and what the MVP must do, which is where most of the time goes.
