At pre-seed, a freelance designer or a short, fixed-scope agency sprint usually fits, because you need speed on a narrow problem, not a design team. From seed to Series A, a specialized UX agency gives you senior range and research depth without a long hiring cycle. From Series B onward, most SaaS companies build an in-house team that owns the core product and keep an outside partner for spikes, specialist work and new bets.

That is the short answer. The longer one depends less on your funding round than on three questions: how fast your roadmap changes, how much of your product's value lives in its interface, and whether anyone on your team can direct design work well. Here is how the options compare, and how the answer shifts as you grow.

## What does each option actually give you?

| | Freelance designer | UX design agency | In-house team |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to start | Fast, if they are free | Fast, often days | Slow, hiring takes months |
| Range of skills | One person's skills | Research, UX, UI, systems, sometimes code | Grows with headcount |
| Product context | Builds slowly | Builds fast with a good brief | Deepest over time |
| Management load | High: you direct the work | Lower: the agency runs the process | High: you build and lead a team |
| Continuity risk | High if they leave | Low: the team covers gaps | Low once the team is stable |
| Flexibility | High | High | Low: fixed headcount |

None of these is better in general. Each one is a bet on where your bottleneck is.

## What fits at pre-seed?

At pre-seed you are trying to learn whether anyone wants the thing. The design job is narrow: a credible MVP, a clickable prototype for customer conversations, a landing page that explains the product. Our [UX design for startups guide](/ux-design-for-startups/) breaks down what matters at each stage.

![Person typing on a laptop at a wooden desk, the setup of a solo freelance designer](../../assets/journal/ux-agency-vs-freelancer-vs-in-house/freelancer-laptop.jpg)

**A freelancer works** when the scope is small, a founder has strong product taste and time to direct the work, and you can live with one person's strengths and blind spots.

**A short agency sprint works** when the MVP is complex (a data-heavy dashboard, a developer tool, a multi-role workflow) or when no founder has design experience. You get research, UX and UI from a team that has done it before, on a fixed scope and date. Our [1to1000 case study](/work/1to1000/) is an example: an AI real estate matching app that went from concept to a market-ready MVP in 45 days.

**In-house rarely fits yet.** A first full-time designer before product and market are clear often ends up as a production resource with no strategy to execute against.

## What fits at seed?

Seed is when the product starts to sprawl. You have early customers, a backlog that changes every week, and the first signs of [UX debt that slows growth](/from-mvp-to-scale-ux-mistakes-that-kill-b2b-saas-growth/): inconsistent screens, onboarding that leaks, features bolted on without a system.

This is where a specialized agency usually earns its place. You need someone to run research with real customers, set up the foundations of a design system and keep pace with engineering, and you probably cannot hire a senior designer who does all three fast enough. A continuous model, where you send requests into a queue instead of renegotiating a scope each time, matches how seed-stage roadmaps behave. We explain why in our piece on [continuous UX for fast-moving teams](/continuous-ux/), and if you go this route, here is [how to choose a UX design agency for B2B SaaS](/how-to-choose-ux-design-agency-saas/), plus our shortlist of [the best UX design agencies for B2B SaaS](/best-ux-design-agencies-b2b-saas/).

A freelancer still works at seed if the product is simple and someone in-house can act as design lead. The risk is continuity: when your one designer gets busy or moves on, the context goes with them.

## What fits from Series A to Series C?

### Series A: start building the core team

After Series A you have the budget and the product clarity to hire. The common pattern is a first senior product designer or design lead in-house, working alongside an agency that keeps throughput up while the team forms. The agency also helps the new hire by documenting the system and decisions so far.

### Series B: in-house owns the core product

By Series B most SaaS companies have a design team with a lead, a few product designers and often a researcher. In-house now owns the core product, because the deep context compounds. An outside partner is still useful for a new product line, a major redesign, a specialist need such as data visualization or motion, or a launch that needs more hands than you have.

### Series C: specialists and spikes

At this stage the in-house team is the default and outside partners fill specific gaps. The question shifts from "who designs the product" to "where do we need more depth or more speed than our team can give right now."

## When should you switch from one model to another?

Watch for these signals:

- **Freelancer to agency:** your designer is the bottleneck, research is not happening, or every new feature looks a little different from the last.
- **Agency to in-house hire:** design decisions need daily presence in product meetings, the context you re-explain each week is growing, and you have a stable roadmap for at least the next year.
- **In-house to adding a partner:** the team is stuck on maintenance while a new product or redesign waits, or you need a skill nobody on the team has.

Switching does not need to be abrupt. The cleanest transitions overlap, so context moves from one group to the next instead of being lost.

Yes, and most growing companies do. A common setup is an in-house design lead who owns the vision and the system, with an agency or an embedded designer adding capacity. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a freelance designer cheaper than an agency?

Not always once you count everything. A freelancer's rate covers one person, while your team supplies the direction, research and review. An agency includes process and range, which reduces the time your founders and engineers spend managing design. Compare the total effort, not only the invoice.

### When should a SaaS startup hire its first in-house designer?

Usually once product direction is stable enough to plan a year ahead and design decisions need someone in the room every day. For many companies that lands around Series A, but the trigger is the workload and the clarity, not the round.

### Can an agency work alongside our in-house designers?

Yes. The best setups share one design system and one decision maker, with the agency taking defined work such as a new module, research or overflow. Ask any agency how they have worked inside an existing team before.

### What is the biggest risk of relying on one freelancer?

Continuity. If they get sick, take another project or leave, the product context leaves with them. Keep files, decisions and components documented in your own tools so the work survives any change in who does it.
